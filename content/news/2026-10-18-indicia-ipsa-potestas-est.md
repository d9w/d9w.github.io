## Making the painting

This painting began as a long written prompt. I described the overall image in
detail, then asked an image-generation model to make the whole painting. Those
first attempts gave me ideas for the layout: where the larger figures might sit,
how groups could be arranged, and how the eye might move across the canvas.

{{< painting-image src="early-composition.png" width="1400" class="painting-study" alt="An early composition with densely grouped fantastical figures, tall structures, and a bright sky at the left." caption="An early whole-image study, before the figures were developed and assembled separately." >}}

From there, the work became a series of conversations about individual figures
and small scenes. I would describe an element, look at the generated image, and
discuss what to change. Some needed another attempt; others were kept and edited.
The process let me work on a pose, an expression, or the arrangement of a group
without having to remake the entire picture each time.

Much of the later work happened in Codex, using image generation and editing
alongside Python scripts for composition. The project kept separate image files
for the figures and groups, together with a layout recording their positions,
sizes, and drawing order. That made it possible to replace one element or move a
scene while keeping the rest of the composition in place.

## Assembling and revising

The separate images were placed onto a large canvas, then reviewed together.
Scale needed several rounds of adjustment. A figure that looked clear on its own
could disappear in the full composition; a group could occupy too much space or
sit awkwardly beside its neighbours. Moving and resizing the elements changed
the balance of the whole painting, so I kept returning to both the full view and
individual details.

Assembly also brought a different set of problems. The source images had their
own backgrounds and lighting. Masks separated the figures from those
backgrounds, while further editing worked on the ground, water, sky, shadows,
and transitions between scenes. Some corrections were made on small sections of
the canvas and blended back into the larger image.

The last rounds were increasingly local: adjusting a ladder or chair, cleaning
up an edge, removing a stray fragment, and making the light continuous across
the scene. I saved successive versions so that changes could be compared and
earlier decisions revisited. The final canvas is 12,800 pixels wide; the large
file linked above preserves the details that are hard to see in the page preview.
