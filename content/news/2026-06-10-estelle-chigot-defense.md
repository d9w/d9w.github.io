Chigot](https://www.linkedin.com/in/estelle-chigot/) successfully defended a
PhD thesis entitled [Image-Based Synthetic-to-Real Domain Adaptation for Robust Object Recognition](https://theses.fr/2026UTLSA021)
(abstract below). This industrial (CIFRE) thesis with Airbus was
directed by Thomas Oberlin with my co-supervision and that of Meriem Ghrib and
Manon Huguenin at Airbus. This thesis demonstrated how synthetic data could be
used in computer vision pipelines in real applications, even the very
challenging one of runway detection in adverse weather conditions. The
theoretical and applicative contributions of this thesis demonstrated Estelle's
pragmatic and decidated approach to research, which was acknowledged by a Best
of Track and 2nd Best of Conference Award at the conference DASC 2026. As of
the writing of this post, Estelle is helping with teaching AI at ISAE-Supaero
and is looking for research opportunities in computer vision and video games.

## Image-Based Synthetic-to-Real Domain Adaptation for Robust Object Recognition

Deep learning models for object recognition require large annotated datasets,
which are costly and difficult to obtain for industrial applications. Synthetic
data generated by simulators offers a scalable alternative with perfect
annotations, but models trained on synthetic images perform poorly on real data
due to the domain gap. This thesis investigates image-based domain adaptation
methods to bridge this synthetic-to-real gap, with a focus on semantic
segmentation under adverse weather conditions. We first propose two
diffusion-based style transfer methods, CACTI and CACTIF, that leverage
semantic segmentation masks to transfer the visual style of real target images
onto synthetic content while preserving structural coherence. Class-wise
Adaptive Instance Normalization aligns feature statistics per semantic class,
and selective attention filtering suppresses artifacts from weak cross-image
correspondences. Experiments on the GTA5 to Cityscapes and ACDC benchmark show
that these methods improve segmentation performance across difficult weather
conditions. We then formulate the selection and ordering of style transfer
operators as a multi-objective optimization problem, MOOSS, solved with
evolutionary algorithms. Using paired-image metrics for efficient evaluation,
the method explores the trade-off between content preservation and style
similarity. While the optimized pipelines produce visually diverse results,
they reveal that current perceptual metrics do not reliably predict downstream
task performance. Finally, we apply these approaches to runway detection for
automatic landing, an industrial use case where real data is scarce and domain
shift is significant. Experiments confirm that style transfer improves
robustness to adverse conditions, while also exposing the limitations of
current benchmarks dominated by urban driving data.
